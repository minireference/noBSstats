E4.17¶

Recall Charlotte's students dataset datasets/students.csv. She wants to compare the scores of students enrolled in the debate and lecture curriculum variants to see if the new teaching method makes a difference. She decides to fit a linear model that uses the categorical variable curriculum as a predictor. Report the results of the \(t\)-test for the slope parameter \(\beta_{\texttt{curriculum}}\) and interpret them.

Hint: Use the formula   "score 1 + C(curriculum)".

Hint: You can get the \(t\)-statistics and \(p\)-values of the parameters from the attributes .tvalues and .pvalues of the model fit result object.

Let's now repeat the calculations of Student's two-sample \(t\)-test with pooled variance from by calling the function scipy.stats.ttest_ind with the option equal_var=True.

Out[13]:
TtestResult(statistic=-1.7197867420465698, pvalue=0.10917234443214319, df=13.0)

We see that the \(t\)-statistic and the \(p\)-value are exactly the same. Indeed, the \(t\)-test for the slope parameter of a binary categorical predictor is equivalent to Student's \(t\)-test with pooled variance.
