One-sample t-test for the mean

The goal of the one-sample ttt-test to check if the mean μ\muμ of an unknown population X∼N(μ,σ0)X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma_0)X∼N(μ,σ0​), equals the mean μ0\mu_0μ0​ of a theoretical distribution X0∼N(μ0,σ0)X_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \sigma_0)X0​∼N(μ0​,σ0​). We’ll estimate the standard deviation σ0\sigma_0σ0​ from the sample standard deviation sx\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{x}}sx​. We discussed this hypothesis test in notebooks/34_analytical_approx.ipynb.

Data¶

One sample of numerical observations x=[x1,x2,…,xn]\mathbf{x}=[x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n]x=[x1​,x2​,…,xn​].

Modeling assumptions¶

We assume the population is normally distributed (NORM)\textbf{(NORM)}(NORM), or the sample is large enough (LARGEn)\textbf{(LARGEn)}(LARGEn).

We also assume that the variance of the unknown population is known and equal to the variance of the theoretical population under H0H_0H0​

Hypotheses¶

H0:μ=μ0H_0: \mu = \mu_0H0​:μ=μ0​ and HA:μ≠μ0H_A: \mu \neq \mu_0HA​:μ=μ0​, where μ\muμ is the unknown population mean, μ0\mu_0μ0​ is the theoretical mean we are comparing to.

Estimates¶

Calculate the sample mean x‾=mean(x)\overline{\mathbf{x}} = \Mean(\mathbf{x})x=mean(x) and the sample standard deviation sx=std(x)s_{\mathbf{x}} = \Std(\mathbf{x})sx​=std(x). Test statistic¶

P-value calculation¶

from ministats import ttest_mean

%psource ttest_mean
def ttest_mean(sample, mu0, alt="two-sided"): """ T-test to detect mean deviation from a population with known mean `mu0`. """ assert alt in ["greater", "less", "two-sided"] obsmean = np.mean(sample) n = len(sample) std = np.std(sample, ddof=1) sehat = std / np.sqrt(n) obst = (obsmean - mu0) / sehat rvT = tdist(df=n-1) pvalue = tailprobs(rvT, obst, alt=alt) return pvalue

To perform the one-sample ttt-test on the sample xs, we call ttest_mean(xs, mu0=...), with ... replaced by the expected mean μX0\mu_{X_0}μX0​​ of the theoretical population under H0H_0H0​

Examples¶

For all the examples we present below, we assume the theoretical distribution we expect under the null hypothesis, is normally distributed with mean μX0=100\mu_{X_0}=100μX0​​=100 and standard deviation σX0=5\sigma_{X_0} = 5σX0​​=5:

rvX0=X0∼N(100,5).\texttt{rvX0} = X_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(100,5).rvX0=X0​∼N(100,5).

To obtain the ppp-value, we first compute the observed ttt-statistic, then calculate the tail probabilities in the two tails of the standard normal distribution T0∼T(ν)T_0 \sim \mathcal{T}(\nu)T0​∼T(ν).

from scipy.stats import t as tdist

obsmean = np.mean(xAs)
std = np.std(xAs, ddof=1)
sehat = std / np.sqrt(n)
obst = (obsmean - muX0) / sehat
rvT0 = tdist(df=n-1)
pvalue = rvT0.cdf(-obst) + 1-rvT0.cdf(obst)
obst, pvalue
(5.413149619618617, 3.186228235607658e-05)

The helper function ttest_mean from the ministats module performs exactly the same sequence of steps to compute the ppp-value.

from ministats import ttest_mean
ttest_mean(xAs, mu0=muX0)
3.1862282356088544e-05

The ppp-value we obtain is 0.00003186, which is below the cutoff value α=0.05\alpha=0.05α=0.05, so our conclusion is we reject the null hypothesis: the mean of the sample xAs is not statistically significantly different from the theoretically expected mean μX0=100\mu_{X_0} = 100μX0​​=100.
