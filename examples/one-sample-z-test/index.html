One-sample z-test for the mean

The goal of the one-sample zzz-test to check if the mean μ\muμ of an unknown population X∼N(μ,σ)X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma)X∼N(μ,σ), equals the mean μ0\mu_0μ0​ of a theoretical distribution X0∼N(μ0,σ)X_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \sigma)X0​∼N(μ0​,σ). Note we assume that standard deviation of the unknown population XXX is known and equal to the standard deviation of the theoretical population σ\sigmaσ. We discussed this hypothesis test in notebooks/34_analytical_approx.ipynb.

Data¶

One sample of numerical observations x=(x1,x2,…,xn)\mathbf{x} = (x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n)x=(x1​,x2​,…,xn​).

Modeling assumptions¶

We model the unknown population as...

and the theoretical distribution is...

We assume the population is normally distributed (NORM)\textbf{(NORM)}(NORM), or the sample is large enough (LARGEn)\textbf{(LARGEn)}(LARGEn).

We also assume that the variance of the unknown population is known and equal to the variance of the theoretical population under H0H_0H0​

Hypotheses¶

H0:μ=μ0H_0: \mu = \mu_0H0​:μ=μ0​ and HA:μ≠μ0H_A: \mu \neq \mu_0HA​:μ=μ0​, where μ\muμ is the unknown population mean, μ0\mu_0μ0​ is the theoretical mean we are comparing to.

Statistical design¶

for n=20n=20n=20 ...

Test statistic¶

Compute z=x‾−μ0σ0/nz = \frac{\overline{\mathbf{x}} - \mu_0}{\sigma_0/\sqrt{n}}z=σ0​/n​x−μ0​​, where x‾\overline{\mathbf{x}}x is the sample mean, μ0\mu_0μ0​ is the theoretical population mean, σ0\sigma_0σ0​ is the known standard deviation.

Sampling distribution¶

Standard normal distribution Z∼N(0,1)Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)Z∼N(0,1).

P-value calculation¶

from ministats import ztest

%psource ztest
def ztest(sample, mu0, sigma0, alt="two-sided"): """ Z-test to detect mean deviation from known normal population. """ mean = np.mean(sample) n = len(sample) se = sigma0 / np.sqrt(n) obsz = (mean - mu0) / se rvZ = norm(0,1) pval = tailprobs(rvZ, obsz, alt=alt) return pval

To perform the one-sample zzz-test on the sample xs, we call ztest(xs, mu0=..., sigma0=...), with the ...s replaced by the mean and standard deviation parameters of the distribution under H0H_0H0​.

For all the examples we present below, we assume the theoretical distribution we expect under the null hypothesis, is normally distributed with mean μX0=100\mu_{X_0}=100μX0​​=100 and standard deviation σX0=5\sigma_{X_0} = 5σX0​​=5:

To obtain the ppp-value, we first compute the observed zzz-statistic, then calculate the tail probabilities in the two tails of the standard normal distribution Z∼N(0,1)Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)Z∼N(0,1).

obsmean = np.mean(xAs)
se = sigmaX0 / np.sqrt(n)
obsz = (obsmean - muX0) / se
rvZ = norm(0,1)
pvalue = rvZ.cdf(-obsz) + 1-rvZ.cdf(obsz)
obsz, pvalue
(3.1180664906014357, 0.0018204172963375287)

The helper function ztest in the ministats module performs exactly the same sequence of steps to compute the ppp-value.

from ministats import ztest
ztest(xAs, mu0=muX0, sigma0=sigmaX0)
0.0018204172963375855

The ppp-value we obtain is 0.0018 (0.18%), which is below the cutoff value α=0.05\alpha=0.05α=0.05, so our conclusion is we reject the null hypothesis: the mean of the sample xAs is not statistically significantly different from the theoretically expected mean μX0=100\mu_{X_0} = 100μX0​​=100.
